TrainClear (the "Service") is operated by Serag Sorror, an individual based in Portland, Oregon ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the TrainClear app and this website collect and why.

1. What We Collect

1.1 Location (on your device, never uploaded)

If you allow location access, the app uses your device's location while it is open to center the map, to show a crossing's status automatically when you approach a crossing with an active alert, and to check that you are within about half a mile of a crossing before you can report it. These checks run on your device. We do not send your coordinates to our servers or keep a location history. A crossing report does reveal that someone was near that crossing at that time (see 1.3). We never request background location. If you deny location access, the app still works.

1.2 Notification settings and push tokens

When you allow notifications, the app stores a push token issued through Expo's push service (which delivers via Google Firebase Cloud Messaging or Apple Push Notification service), together with your notification settings: which crossings, which train types (freight, MAX, Amtrak/WES, and for how long), quiet hours, your platform and the app version. These are stored under a random install ID (1.7). The token contains no name, number or location. If the push service reports that the token is no longer valid (for example after you uninstall), we clear it.

1.3 Crossing reports

If you submit a report ("train here" / "all clear"), we store the crossing, the report type, the time, and, if you choose, whether it was a freight or passenger train. A copy visible to other app users contains no identifier at all. A separate, non-public copy carries a random per-install report token, used only to tell a second, different reporter apart from the same person tapping twice. Reports are never linked to your name or email.

1.4 Payments

TrainClear is currently free and has no in-app purchases. If we add paid features, payments will be handled by Apple or Google and we will update this policy before that happens.

1.5 Usage analytics and diagnostics

We send a small set of in-app events to Google Analytics, such as which crossing you opened, whether you opened a detour, tapped a notification, or saw a proximity pop-up. They are tagged with the random install ID, never your name or email. Google derives an approximate (city-level) region from your IP address. We do not currently collect crash reports ourselves; if we start, we will update this policy first. On Android, the Google Maps component collects device information, crash data and a pseudonymous identifier under Google's own privacy policy. If you have opted in to sharing diagnostics with Apple or Google, they may share crash data with us under their own terms.

1.6 Email

The app has no accounts. We collect your email only if you choose to contact us, or if you sign up for beta news on this website (1.8).

1.7 Install ID

On first launch the app creates a random identifier. It is not derived from your device's hardware IDs or advertising ID, and it resets if you reinstall. It lets us store your notification settings and count app usage without knowing who you are.

1.8 Website beta sign-up

If you sign up for beta news on this website, we receive the name, email address and role (such as commuter or cyclist) you enter, through our form provider (Formspree), and use them only to send you TrainClear beta invites and launch news.

2. What We Do NOT Collect

  • No continuous or background GPS tracking, and no location history.
  • No name, email, or phone number is needed to use the app.
  • No payment card data — ever.
  • No contacts, photos, microphone audio, or camera data from your device.
  • No advertising identifiers, no third-party ad SDKs, no cross-app tracking.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for targeted advertising.

4. Public Data Sources We Process

TrainClear's predictions are built from public and licensed feeds that do not involve your personal data:

  • Publicly broadcast railroad radio (unencrypted defect-detector and road-channel audio). No user data is sent to or received from these sources.
  • Public ODOT/City of Portland traffic cameras. These cameras show public roadways and may incidentally capture vehicles or pedestrians in public view. We process this imagery automatically to detect trains and gate states; we do not use it to identify individuals, read plates, or track anyone.
  • Amtrak public train-location API and TriMet public GTFS-RT feeds. No user data is exchanged.
  • Licensed commercial traffic-flow data. Aggregate road-speed data; no TrainClear user data is provided to these vendors.

We also keep frames from those public traffic cameras for a limited time and review them, partly with automated AI tools provided by service providers (for example Anthropic), to measure how accurate TrainClear is. These images come only from public cameras. No images, audio or data from your device are ever used.

5. Sharing and Disclosure

We do not sell personal information. We share information only:

  • With service providers that process data on our behalf (Google: Firebase database, Google Analytics, Google Maps; Expo: push delivery and app updates; Formspree: website beta sign-ups; Hetzner and Oracle Cloud: server hosting; Anthropic: automated review of public camera images only, never user data), bound to use it only for our purposes.
  • As aggregated, anonymized data. We may share aggregated crossing and prediction statistics (never personal information) with partners such as emergency services or logistics companies. This data cannot reasonably be used to identify you.
  • For legal reasons, if required by law, regulation, legal process, or enforceable governmental request, or to protect the rights, property, or safety of any person.
  • In a business transfer (for example, if TrainClear becomes or is acquired by a company), in which case this policy continues to apply to previously collected data.
